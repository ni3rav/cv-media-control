Gesture Media Controller

Your hand is the remote.

Sit at the laptop, start the music, and clench a fist in front of the webcam until the track pauses. Everything after that is the same idea with a different pose.

Recognition runs on this computer. Nothing about the picture leaves the machine. You get a preview window, a tray icon, and quiet notifications.

Set up once

Work in the project folder. If .venv is already there, skip to the check.

cd ~/code/cv-music-control
uv python install 3.12
uv venv --python 3.12 .venv
uv pip install -e ".[dev]"
.venv/bin/python scripts/fetch_model.py

The model file lands in models/ and is not committed. MediaPipe brings its own OpenCV. Do not install opencv-python beside it.

On Fedora the system packages are playerctl, mpv-libs, wireplumber, and mpv. Debian and Ubuntu use playerctl, wireplumber, mpv, and libmpv2. playerctl talks to VLC, Spotify, and browsers. mpv-libs is the built-in player.

Check the laptop

source .venv/bin/activate
gesture-media doctor

Read the lines. The command exits 0 when every line marked hard has passed. A warning is information, not a failure.

What you seeWhat to do
Camera passedThe webcam opened and was released.
Model and MediaPipe passedRecognition can run.
playerctl, wpctl, and libmpv passedYou can drive an open player and the built-in one.
No active MPRIS playersNothing is playing yet. Start VLC, Spotify, or a browser video, or use the built-in player in the next step.
No calibration profileLeave it. The defaults work. Calibration is a later, optional step.

If the camera you want is not index 0, list the ones that open:

gesture-media list-cameras

Start the music

You have two paths. Pick one before you raise your hand.

Control something that is already playing. Open VLC, Spotify, or a browser tab with sound. Confirm the name shows up:

playerctl -l

Or let the app play files itself. Pitch control only exists on this path. Pass one or more files:

gesture-media run --player mpv --media song.mp3

If you omit --player, the app looks for an MPRIS player and uses it. With none running, it falls through to mpv. --player mpris and --player mpv force the choice.

Volume follows the system output for VLC, Spotify, and the browser. It follows the mpv volume when mpv is the player. That split is intentional. Change it in config.yaml with audio.volume_target set to system or player.

Open the preview

gesture-media run

A window titled Gesture Media Controller opens. A rectangle marks the area where your hand counts. Keep the palm inside it. The picture is mirrored: the hand on your right appears on the right side of the window.

The first launch with no saved profile prints one line about calibration. You can ignore it.

A second launch tells you the app is already running and exits. Quit with q, Esc, Ctrl+C, or the tray Quit item. Quitting releases the camera and closes the built-in player.

If the window never appears on Wayland, start it through XWayland:

QT_QPA_PLATFORM=xcb gesture-media run

Hide the window and keep control from the tray:

gesture-media run --no-preview

Pause a track with a closed fist

Face the camera. Clench one fist and hold it still inside the rectangle. Count about a third of a second. The track toggles between play and pause, a line appears in the terminal, and a notification names the pose.

Drop the hand. The same pose will not fire again until you have let go and a short cooldown has passed. That pause is there so a pose you are still holding does not retrigger.

If nothing happens, look at the overlay. It names the pose it thinks it sees, the score, and a hold bar. A score under 0.6 is ignored. A hand outside the rectangle is ignored. Better light on the hand usually fixes a low score.

Learn the other poses

Discrete poses fire once after you hold them. Volume and seek work like a joystick: the pose locks in, then you move from the spot where your palm started. A small motion inside the deadzone does nothing. Up means toward the top of the frame.

Some players treat previous as “start this track again” once you are a few seconds in. The app sends the ordinary previous command. The player decides what that means.

Point the thumb to your right and hold it to skip ahead. Point it to your left and hold it to go back. Drop the hand and show the palm again to unmute. Thumb up also turns the sound back on if a mute left it silent.

Use the preview and the tray

KeyWhat happens
q or EscQuit and release the camera.
dTurn detection on or off.
hHide or show the overlay.
lDraw the hand landmarks.

The overlay shows the pose, its score, hold progress, the last action, frames per second, volume, mute, and which backend is active. After a calibration profile loads, it also says the profile is active.

The tray icon is green while detection is on, amber while it is paused, and red if the camera is lost. Open it for pause, show or hide the preview, calibrate, open config.yaml, notification settings, and quit.

Every action can post a silent notification. Volume and seek update one popup while you hold the pose, then show the resulting value when you let go. If your hand never left the deadzone, there is no final popup. From the tray you can limit this to start, stop, pause, and errors, or turn it off.

Rest your hands

Hold both fists up for about a second and a half. Detection pauses, the icon turns amber, and a notification says so. Repeat the pose to resume.

The camera stays open at a slower rate, and its light stays on. Only the two-fist pose is watched. To drop the camera instead, set this and then resume from the tray, because the hands are no longer visible:

tray:
  release_camera_when_paused: true

Tune it to your seat

Skip this until the defaults feel wrong for your distance or lighting. It takes about a minute and never runs unless you ask.

gesture-media calibrate
gesture-media calibrate --skip-gestures
gesture-media calibrate --reset

The window asks you to hold a relaxed hand still, then move to a comfortable top, bottom, left, and right. Unless you skipped them, it then asks for each of the five poses. Esc throws the session away and writes nothing.

At the end it prints the old and new deadzone, reach, and arming zone, and asks whether to save. Answer y or n in the window or the terminal. A saved profile is ~/.config/gesture-media/profile.yaml. The next run uses it. Flags such as --camera and --no-calibration still win over the file.

Order of settings, from weakest to strongest: built-in defaults, then config.yaml, then the profile, then the command you typed.

Record a session and play it back

Recording stores poses and landmark coordinates. It does not store pictures.

gesture-media record --out run.jsonl
gesture-media replay run.jsonl

Replay runs the recording through the gesture logic and a fake player, then prints the actions. Your real volume and your real player stay where they are. Use it when you want to see what a session would have done.

--verbose on a normal run prints a debug line per frame. Frames are still not written anywhere.

Fix what you see

What happenedWhat to do
The camera will not openQuit anything else using the webcam. Run gesture-media list-cameras, then gesture-media run --camera N. A failed open exits with code 2.
The music ignores youStart a player and check playerctl -l, or run with --player mpv --media file.
The preview is a blank windowQT_QPA_PLATFORM=xcb gesture-media run
Pitch does nothingVLC and Spotify cannot do it. Use --player mpv.
The pose flickers or never settlesPut more light on the hand, or raise vision.min_score and engine.stable_ms in config.yaml. Calibrate in the chair you actually use.
The camera light stays on after two fistsThat is the paused state. Quit, or set release_camera_when_paused.

Day to day, the whole loop is: start the music, run gesture-media run, hold the pose until the notification agrees with you, quit when you are done.

Settings you will edit live in config.yaml beside this file. Hold times, the deadzone, the camera index, and the command that opens a player are all there.
